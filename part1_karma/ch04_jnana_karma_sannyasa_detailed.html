ॐ Chapter 4 — Jñāna-Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga ॐ

ज्ञानकर्मसंन्यासयोगः : జ్ఞానకర్మసంన్యాసయోగము
"The Yoga of Renunciation of Action through Knowledge"  ·  42 verses  ·  Detailed verse-by-verse edition with Ādi-Śaṅkara's Bhāṣya
Having taught karma-yoga in Chapter 3, Kṛṣṇa now reveals its ancient lineage and its true fruit — knowledge (jñāna). The title captures Śaṅkara's whole thesis: this chapter is about the renunciation of action through knowledge.

Its climax (4.18–4.24, 4.33, 4.37–4.38) is the doctrine that all action culminates in knowledge, and that the wise one "sees inaction in action." The renunciation taught here is internal and epistemic: one does not necessarily drop the activity — one drops the false sense of agency ("I am the doer") by knowing the Self to be the actionless witness.

§ 1 · The lineage of this yoga & the doctrine of avatāra (4.1–4.8)

BG 4.1An imperishable, royal lineage
श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
इमं विवस्वते योगं प्रोक्तवानहमव्ययम् ।
विवस्वान्मनवे प्राह मनुरिक्ष्वाकवेऽब्रवीत् ॥ ४.१ ॥
śrī-bhagavān uvāca | imaṁ vivasvate yogaṁ proktavān aham avyayam |
vivasvān manave prāha manur ikṣvākave 'bravīt ||
The Lord: "I taught this imperishable yoga to Vivasvān (the Sun); Vivasvān told it to Manu; Manu told it to Ikṣvāku."
Bhāṣya: This yoga is ancient and unbroken — passed down a royal lineage (rāja-ṛṣi paramparā). Its transmission through great kings underscores its authority and its suitability for those engaged in action, not only recluses.
BG 4.2Lost over the long course of time
एवं परम्पराप्राप्तमिमं राजर्षयो विदुः ।
स कालेनेह महता योगो नष्टः परन्तप ॥ ४.२ ॥
evaṁ paramparā-prāptam imaṁ rājarṣayo viduḥ |
sa kāleneha mahatā yogo naṣṭaḥ parantapa ||
"Thus received in succession, the royal sages knew it. But in the long course of time, that yoga was lost, O scorcher of foes."
Bhāṣya: Through weak custodians and inattention the teaching decayed — which is why Kṛṣṇa must restate it now.
BG 4.3The supreme secret, given to the fit
स एवायं मया तेऽद्य योगः प्रोक्तः पुरातनः ।
भक्तोऽसि मे सखा चेति रहस्यं ह्येतदुत्तमम् ॥ ४.३ ॥
sa evāyaṁ mayā te 'dya yogaḥ proktaḥ purātanaḥ |
bhakto 'si me sakhā ceti rahasyaṁ hy etad uttamam ||
"That same ancient yoga I have declared to you today, for you are My devotee and friend; this is the supreme secret."
Bhāṣya: Fitness matters. The teaching is given because Arjuna is a devotee and friend — qualified by devotion and nearness. This knowledge is a "supreme secret," granted only to the fit.
BG 4.4Arjuna's doubt about time
अर्जुन उवाच ।
अपरं भवतो जन्म परं जन्म विवस्वतः ।
कथमेतद्विजानीयां त्वमादौ प्रोक्तवानिति ॥ ४.४ ॥
arjuna uvāca | aparaṁ bhavato janma paraṁ janma vivasvataḥ |
katham etad vijānīyāṁ tvam ādau proktavān iti ||
Arjuna: "Your birth was later; Vivasvān's was earlier. How am I to understand that You taught this in the beginning?"
Bhāṣya: A natural doubt, taking Kṛṣṇa as an ordinary man recently born. This objection sets up the great teaching on divine incarnation.
BG 4.5The Lord knows all births; the jīva forgets
श्रीभगवानुवाच ।
बहूनि मे व्यतीतानि जन्मानि तव चार्जुन ।
तान्यहं वेद सर्वाणि न त्वं वेत्थ परन्तप ॥ ४.५ ॥
śrī-bhagavān uvāca | bahūni me vyatītāni janmāni tava cārjuna |
tāny ahaṁ veda sarvāṇi na tvaṁ vettha parantapa ||
The Lord: "Many births of Mine have passed, and of yours too, Arjuna. I know them all; you do not, O scorcher of foes."
Bhāṣya: The difference between the Lord and the jīva: the Lord, ever-pure and free of avidyā, knows all His "births"; the bound soul, veiled by ignorance, forgets. The Lord's "birth" is not real bondage but māyā.
BG 4.6⭐ Unborn, yet appearing through His own māyā
अजोऽपि सन्नव्ययात्मा भूतानामीश्वरोऽपि सन् ।
प्रकृतिं स्वामधिष्ठाय सम्भवाम्यात्ममायया ॥ ४.६ ॥
ajo 'pi sann avyayātmā bhūtānām īśvaro 'pi san |
prakṛtiṁ svām adhiṣṭhāya sambhavāmy ātma-māyayā ||
"Though I am unborn, of imperishable nature, and the Lord of all beings, yet governing My own prakṛti, I come into being by My own māyā."
Bhāṣya: Crucial for Advaita. The Lord's incarnation is apparent, not a real modification. He is truly unborn and changeless; He "appears" to take birth through His own māyā (creative power), not through karma like ordinary beings. No real loss of His nature.
BG 4.7⭐ Whenever dharma declines, I manifest
यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत ।
अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥ ४.७ ॥
yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata |
abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham ||
"Whenever there is a decline of dharma and a rise of adharma, O Bhārata, then I manifest Myself."
Bhāṣya: The famous avatāra verse. When righteousness wanes and unrighteousness prevails, the Lord "sends forth" Himself — again, through māyā — to restore balance.
BG 4.8⭐ To protect the good, destroy evil, re-establish dharma
परित्राणाय साधूनां विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् ।
धर्मसंस्थापनार्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे ॥ ४.८ ॥
paritrāṇāya sādhūnāṁ vināśāya ca duṣkṛtām |
dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya sambhavāmi yuge yuge ||
"For the protection of the good, the destruction of evildoers, and the re-establishment of dharma, I come into being age after age."
Bhāṣya: The threefold purpose of incarnation. Śaṅkara notes "protection of the good" (those who tread the right path) is the primary aim.

§ 2 · Knowing the Lord; who reaches Him; how the ancients acted (4.9–4.15)

BG 4.9Knowing His divine birth & action liberates
जन्म कर्म च मे दिव्यमेवं यो वेत्ति तत्त्वतः ।
त्यक्त्वा देहं पुनर्जन्म नैति मामेति सोऽर्जुन ॥ ४.९ ॥
janma karma ca me divyam evaṁ yo vetti tattvataḥ |
tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti mām eti so 'rjuna ||
"He who thus knows, in truth, the divine birth and action of Mine, on leaving the body is not born again but comes to Me, O Arjuna."
Bhāṣya: Right knowledge of the Lord's birth-and-action as divine (illusory, māyā-born, not karmically forced) is itself liberating. Such a knower is not reborn — he attains the Lord. Knowledge, not ritual, liberates.
BG 4.10Purified by the austerity of knowledge
वीतरागभयक्रोधा मन्मया मामुपाश्रिताः ।
बहवो ज्ञानतपसा पूता मद्भावमागताः ॥ ४.१० ॥
vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhā man-mayā mām upāśritāḥ |
bahavo jñāna-tapasā pūtā mad-bhāvam āgatāḥ ||
"Freed from attachment, fear, and anger, absorbed in Me, taking refuge in Me, purified by the austerity of knowledge, many have attained My being."
Bhāṣya: The qualifications for liberation: free of rāga, bhaya, krodha; wholly absorbed in the Lord; purified by jñāna-tapas (knowledge as austerity). "Attaining My being" = becoming one with Brahman.
BG 4.11As they approach Me, so I favor them
ये यथा मां प्रपद्यन्ते तांस्तथैव भजाम्यहम् ।
मम वर्त्मानुवर्तन्ते मनुष्याः पार्थ सर्वशः ॥ ४.११ ॥
ye yathā māṁ prapadyante tāṁs tathaiva bhajāmy aham |
mama vartmānuvartante manuṣyāḥ pārtha sarvaśaḥ ||
"In whatever way people approach Me, so do I favor them; men follow My path in all ways, O Pārtha."
Bhāṣya: The Lord responds to each according to their approach and desire — impartial, granting fruits in proportion to worship. All human paths ultimately lead toward Him.
BG 4.12Quick but perishable fruit of ritual
काङ्क्षन्तः कर्मणां सिद्धिं यजन्त इह देवताः ।
क्षिप्रं हि मानुषे लोके सिद्धिर्भवति कर्मजा ॥ ४.१२ ॥
kāṅkṣantaḥ karmaṇāṁ siddhiṁ yajanta iha devatāḥ |
kṣipraṁ hi mānuṣe loke siddhir bhavati karma-jā ||
"Desiring success in action, people here worship the gods; for quickly in the human world does success born of action come."
Bhāṣya: Those craving quick worldly results worship deities and gain speedy but limited, action-born fruits — contrasted with the higher, imperishable pursuit of knowledge.
BG 4.13⭐ Author of the varṇas, yet the changeless non-doer
चातुर्वर्ण्यं मया सृष्टं गुणकर्मविभागशः ।
तस्य कर्तारमपि मां विद्ध्यकर्तारमव्ययम् ॥ ४.१३ ॥
cātur-varṇyaṁ mayā sṛṣṭaṁ guṇa-karma-vibhāgaśaḥ |
tasya kartāram api māṁ viddhy akartāram avyayam ||
"The four varṇas were created by Me according to the distribution of guṇas and actions. Though I am their author, know Me to be the non-doer, the changeless."
Bhāṣya: A key Advaita point. The Lord "creates" the fourfold order through the guṇas of prakṛti — yet He is truly the non-doer (akartā) and immutable. He acts "through māyā" while remaining actionless — the same non-agency the sage realizes for himself.
BG 4.14⭐ No craving for fruit, so no taint — the secret of freedom-in-action
न मां कर्माणि लिम्पन्ति न मे कर्मफले स्पृहा ।
इति मां योऽभिजानाति कर्मभिर्न स बध्यते ॥ ४.१४ ॥
na māṁ karmāṇi limpanti na me karma-phale spṛhā |
iti māṁ yo 'bhijānāti karmabhir na sa badhyate ||
"Actions do not taint Me, nor have I any craving for their fruits. He who knows Me thus is not bound by actions."
Bhāṣya: Because the Lord has no desire for results, actions do not "stain" Him. The liberating lesson: one who knows and lives this — acting without craving for fruit, like the Lord — is likewise not bound.
BG 4.15The ancient seekers acted with this knowledge
एवं ज्ञात्वा कृतं कर्म पूर्वैरपि मुमुक्षुभिः ।
कुरु कर्मैव तस्मात्त्वं पूर्वैः पूर्वतरं कृतम् ॥ ४.१५ ॥
evaṁ jñātvā kṛtaṁ karma pūrvair api mumukṣubhiḥ |
kuru karmaiva tasmāt tvaṁ pūrvaiḥ pūrvataraṁ kṛtam ||
"Knowing this, the ancient seekers of liberation performed action. Therefore you too should perform action, as done by the ancients in olden times."
Bhāṣya: Even ancient mumukṣus (seekers of liberation) acted — with this knowledge, as a purifying discipline. So Arjuna should likewise act, in the time-honored way, as a means to purity and knowledge.

§ 3 · The heart of the chapter — "inaction in action" (4.16–4.24)

BG 4.16Even the wise are confused about action
किं कर्म किमकर्मेति कवयोऽप्यत्र मोहिताः ।
तत्ते कर्म प्रवक्ष्यामि यज्ज्ञात्वा मोक्ष्यसेऽशुभात् ॥ ४.१६ ॥
kiṁ karma kim akarmeti kavayo 'py atra mohitāḥ |
tat te karma pravakṣyāmi yaj jñātvā mokṣyase 'śubhāt ||
"What is action? What is inaction? Even the wise are confused about this. I shall explain that action, knowing which you will be freed from evil."
Bhāṣya: A subtle, difficult topic — so subtle that even the learned are deluded. Right understanding of karma / akarma liberates from aśubha (evil, i.e., bondage/saṁsāra).
BG 4.17Deep is the path of action
कर्मणो ह्यपि बोद्धव्यं बोद्धव्यं च विकर्मणः ।
अकर्मणश्च बोद्धव्यं गहना कर्मणो गतिः ॥ ४.१७ ॥
karmaṇo hy api boddhavyaṁ boddhavyaṁ ca vikarmaṇaḥ |
akarmaṇaś ca boddhavyaṁ gahanā karmaṇo gatiḥ ||
"One must understand action, and also forbidden action (vikarma), and inaction; deep is the path of action."
Bhāṣya: Three things must be discerned: karma (right action), vikarma (prohibited action), and akarma (inaction). The truth about action is profound (gahanā) — the deepest point being to recognize the actionless Self within action.
BG 4.18⭐⭐ The summit: seeing inaction in action
कर्मण्यकर्म यः पश्येदकर्मणि च कर्म यः ।
स बुद्धिमान्मनुष्येषु स युक्तः कृत्स्नकर्मकृत् ॥ ४.१८ ॥
karmaṇy akarma yaḥ paśyed akarmaṇi ca karma yaḥ |
sa buddhimān manuṣyeṣu sa yuktaḥ kṛtsna-karma-kṛt ||
"He who sees inaction in action, and action in inaction, is wise among men; he is a yogin who has done all action."
Bhāṣya: The summit of the chapter. The Self is actionless; all action belongs to prakṛti. The wise one sees inaction in action — the Self does nothing even while the body works; and action in inaction — the ego, even while "doing nothing," is still falsely engaged in agency. This seeing is liberating knowledge itself. (The epistemic renunciation.)
BG 4.19⭐ Actions burnt in the fire of knowledge
यस्य सर्वे समारम्भाः कामसङ्कल्पवर्जिताः ।
ज्ञानाग्निदग्धकर्माणं तमाहुः पण्डितं बुधाः ॥ ४.१९ ॥
yasya sarve samārambhāḥ kāma-saṅkalpa-varjitāḥ |
jñānāgni-dagdha-karmāṇaṁ tam āhuḥ paṇḍitaṁ budhāḥ ||
"He whose undertakings are all free from desire and selfish intention, whose actions are burnt in the fire of knowledge — him the wise call a sage (paṇḍita)."
Bhāṣya: The sage's actions, done without desire or saṅkalpa (willful intent for results), are "burnt in the fire of knowledge" — their binding potency destroyed by the realization "I am not the doer."
BG 4.20⭐ Busy in action, yet "does nothing at all"
त्यक्त्वा कर्मफलासङ्गं नित्यतृप्तो निराश्रयः ।
कर्मण्यभिप्रवृत्तोऽपि नैव किञ्चित्करोति सः ॥ ४.२० ॥
tyaktvā karma-phalāsaṅgaṁ nitya-tṛpto nirāśrayaḥ |
karmaṇy abhipravṛtto 'pi naiva kiñcit karoti saḥ ||
"Having abandoned attachment to the fruits of action, ever content, dependent on nothing — though fully engaged in action, he does nothing at all."
Bhāṣya: Even while outwardly busy, the knower "does nothing," having renounced doership and desire for fruit. Nitya-tṛpta — ever-content, needing no external support. The clearest statement of freedom-in-action.
BG 4.21Action merely with the body, no sin
निराशीर्यतचित्तात्मा त्यक्तसर्वपरिग्रहः ।
शारीरं केवलं कर्म कुर्वन्नाप्नोति किल्बिषम् ॥ ४.२१ ॥
nirāśīr yata-cittātmā tyakta-sarva-parigrahaḥ |
śārīraṁ kevalaṁ karma kurvan nāpnoti kilbiṣam ||
"Free from expectation, mind and self controlled, having given up all possessions, performing action merely with the body, he incurs no sin."
Bhāṣya: The knower, desiring nothing, self-controlled, non-possessive, performs only bodily action (for mere maintenance, without egoistic investment). Such action accrues no sin/bondage.
BG 4.22Content with what comes unsought
यदृच्छालाभसन्तुष्टो द्वन्द्वातीतो विमत्सरः ।
समः सिद्धावसिद्धौ च कृत्वापि न निबध्यते ॥ ४.२२ ॥
yadṛcchā-lābha-santuṣṭo dvandvātīto vimatsaraḥ |
samaḥ siddhāv asiddhau ca kṛtvāpi na nibadhyate ||
"Content with whatever comes unsought, beyond the pairs of opposites, free from envy, even-minded in success and failure — though he acts, he is not bound."
Bhāṣya: The marks of the free sage: satisfaction with whatever comes by chance, transcendence of dualities, no envy, equanimity in gain and loss. Acting from such a state cannot bind.
BG 4.23Action dissolves entirely
गतसङ्गस्य मुक्तस्य ज्ञानावस्थितचेतसः ।
यज्ञायाचरतः कर्म समग्रं प्रविलीयते ॥ ४.२३ ॥
gata-saṅgasya muktasya jñānāvasthita-cetasaḥ |
yajñāyācarataḥ karma samagraṁ pravilīyate ||
"For one free from attachment, liberated, with mind established in knowledge, acting for the sake of sacrifice — his action dissolves entirely."
Bhāṣya: For the jñāna-niṣṭha (established in knowledge) who acts only as yajña (offering, without ego), the entire karma — its residue and binding power — dissolves away.
BG 4.24⭐ All is Brahman — offering, oblation, fire, offerer
ब्रह्मार्पणं ब्रह्म हविर्ब्रह्माग्नौ ब्रह्मणा हुतम् ।
ब्रह्मैव तेन गन्तव्यं ब्रह्मकर्मसमाधिना ॥ ४.२४ ॥
brahmārpaṇaṁ brahma havir brahmāgnau brahmaṇā hutam |
brahmaiva tena gantavyaṁ brahma-karma-samādhinā ||
"The offering is Brahman; the oblation is Brahman; offered by Brahman into the fire of Brahman. He who thus sees all as Brahman reaches Brahman alone."
Bhāṣya: The supreme vision of the knower-of-Brahman: the act of offering, the offered, the fire, and the offerer are all Brahman. For one absorbed in this "action-that-is-Brahman" (brahma-karma-samādhi), the goal attained can only be Brahman. Action is wholly transmuted into knowledge.

§ 4 · The many kinds of yajña; knowledge as the highest (4.25–4.33)

BG 4.25Ritual sacrifice vs. sacrifice into Brahman
दैवमेवापरे यज्ञं योगिनः पर्युपासते ।
ब्रह्माग्नावपरे यज्ञं यज्ञेनैवोपजुह्वति ॥ ४.२५ ॥
daivam evāpare yajñaṁ yoginaḥ paryupāsate |
brahmāgnāv apare yajñaṁ yajñenaivopajuhvati ||
"Some yogins offer sacrifice to the gods; others offer the very self as sacrifice into the fire of Brahman."
Bhāṣya: He begins listing varied "sacrifices." Some perform ritual worship of deities (karma-yogins); others (knowers) offer the self into Brahman — realizing the identity of self and Brahman. The second is the sacrifice of knowledge.
BG 4.26Senses into restraint; objects into the senses
श्रोत्रादीनीन्द्रियाण्यन्ये संयमाग्निषु जुह्वति ।
शब्दादीन्विषयानन्य इन्द्रियाग्निषु जुह्वति ॥ ४.२६ ॥
śrotrādīnīndriyāṇy anye saṁyamāgniṣu juhvati |
śabdādīn viṣayān anya indriyāgniṣu juhvati ||
"Some offer hearing and the other senses into the fires of restraint; others offer sound and the other sense-objects into the fires of the senses."
Bhāṣya: Two more "sacrifices": ascetics who offer the senses into the fire of self-restraint (withdrawing them); and others who, unattached, offer sense-objects into the senses (engaging sensibly without bondage).
BG 4.27Sense- and prāṇa-activity into the fire of self-restraint
सर्वाणीन्द्रियकर्माणि प्राणकर्माणि चापरे ।
आत्मसंयमयोगाग्नौ जुह्वति ज्ञानदीपिते ॥ ४.२७ ॥
sarvāṇīndriya-karmāṇi prāṇa-karmāṇi cāpare |
ātma-saṁyama-yogāgnau juhvati jñāna-dīpite ||
"Others offer all the functions of the senses and of the vital breath into the fire of the yoga of self-restraint, kindled by knowledge."
Bhāṣya: The yogins who still all sense- and prāṇa-activity in the fire of ātma-saṁyama-yoga (self-control), lit by knowledge — advanced meditators whose disciplined mind is illumined by wisdom. (Note the phrase ātma-saṁyama — Śaṅkara's name for Chapter 6.)
BG 4.28Wealth, austerity, yoga, study, knowledge
द्रव्ययज्ञास्तपोयज्ञा योगयज्ञास्तथापरे ।
स्वाध्यायज्ञानयज्ञाश्च यतयः संशितव्रताः ॥ ४.२८ ॥
dravya-yajñās tapo-yajñā yoga-yajñās tathāpare |
svādhyāya-jñāna-yajñāś ca yatayaḥ saṁśita-vratāḥ ||
"Some offer wealth as sacrifice, some austerity, some yoga; others, ascetics of firm vows, offer scriptural study and knowledge as sacrifice."
Bhāṣya: A survey of yajñas: giving (dravya), austerity (tapas), yogic practice, study (svādhyāya), and knowledge (jñāna) — all "sacrifices" by strict-vowed seekers, leading toward purification.
BG 4.29Prāṇāyāma as sacrifice
अपाने जुह्वति प्राणं प्राणेऽपानं तथापरे ।
प्राणापानगती रुद्ध्वा प्राणायामपरायणाः ॥ ४.२९ ॥
apāne juhvati prāṇaṁ prāṇe 'pānaṁ tathāpare |
prāṇāpāna-gatī ruddhvā prāṇāyāma-parāyaṇāḥ ||
"Others, devoted to prāṇāyāma, offer the outgoing breath into the incoming, and the incoming into the outgoing, restraining the flow of both."
Bhāṣya: The prāṇāyāma practitioners' "sacrifice": regulating and offering prāṇa and apāna into one another (pūraka, recaka, kumbhaka). Breath-control as a form of yajña.
BG 4.30All these are knowers of yajña, cleansed of sin
अपरे नियताहाराः प्राणान्प्राणेषु जुह्वति ।
सर्वेऽप्येते यज्ञविदो यज्ञक्षपितकल्मषाः ॥ ४.३० ॥
apare niyatāhārāḥ prāṇān prāṇeṣu juhvati |
sarve 'py ete yajña-vido yajña-kṣapita-kalmaṣāḥ ||
"Others, of regulated diet, offer the vital breaths into the vital breaths. All these are knowers of sacrifice, their impurities destroyed by sacrifice."
Bhāṣya: Concluding the list: all these are "knowers of yajña," and by their sacrifices their sins/impurities are consumed. Purification is the common fruit.
BG 4.31The remnant-nectar leads to Brahman
यज्ञशिष्टामृतभुजो यान्ति ब्रह्म सनातनम् ।
नायं लोकोऽस्त्ययज्ञस्य कुतोऽन्यः कुरुसत्तम ॥ ४.३१ ॥
yajña-śiṣṭāmṛta-bhujo yānti brahma sanātanam |
nāyaṁ loko 'sty ayajñasya kuto 'nyaḥ kuru-sattama ||
"Those who eat the nectar-remnant of sacrifice go to the eternal Brahman. This world is not for the non-sacrificer — how then the other, O best of the Kurus?"
Bhāṣya: Those who partake of the "immortal remnant" of yajña (its purifying fruit) reach Brahman. One who performs no sacrifice gains neither this world nor the next. Yajña is indispensable to any good.
BG 4.32All yajñas are born of action
एवं बहुविधा यज्ञा वितता ब्रह्मणो मुखे ।
कर्मजान्विद्धि तान्सर्वानेवं ज्ञात्वा विमोक्ष्यसे ॥ ४.३२ ॥
evaṁ bahu-vidhā yajñā vitatā brahmaṇo mukhe |
karma-jān viddhi tān sarvān evaṁ jñātvā vimokṣyase ||
"Thus many kinds of sacrifice are spread out in the mouth (revelation) of Brahman. Know them all to be born of action; knowing this, you will be liberated."
Bhāṣya: All these varied yajñas are sanctioned by scripture and are born of action (of body, speech, mind). Realizing that they are all action (means, not the goal), and that the goal is knowledge, one is liberated.
BG 4.33⭐⭐ All action culminates in knowledge
श्रेयान्द्रव्यमयाद्यज्ञाज्ज्ञानयज्ञः परन्तप ।
सर्वं कर्माखिलं पार्थ ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते ॥ ४.३३ ॥
śreyān dravya-mayād yajñāj jñāna-yajñaḥ parantapa |
sarvaṁ karmākhilaṁ pārtha jñāne parisamāpyate ||
"Better than the sacrifice of material objects is the sacrifice of knowledge, O scorcher of foes. All action, without exception, culminates in knowledge, O Pārtha."
Bhāṣya: The decisive verdict. The jñāna-yajña (sacrifice of knowledge) surpasses all material sacrifices. And the pivotal line — "sarvaṁ karmākhilaṁ… jñāne parisamāpyate" — all action culminates in knowledge. Action is preparatory; knowledge is the goal in which action terminates. This is the doctrinal spine of Śaṅkara's whole reading.

§ 5 · How to gain knowledge; its power; the call to act (4.34–4.42)

BG 4.34⭐ Approach a teacher: reverence, inquiry, service
तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया ।
उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥ ४.३४ ॥
tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā |
upadekṣyanti te jñānaṁ jñāninas tattva-darśinaḥ ||
"Know that by prostration, by inquiry, and by service; the wise, the seers of truth, will teach you knowledge."
Bhāṣya: The method for gaining knowledge: approach a realized teacher (tattva-darśī) with (1) humble reverence (praṇipāta), (2) sincere questioning (paripraśna), and (3) service (sevā). Knowledge comes through a qualified guru, not on one's own.
BG 4.35Seeing all beings in the Self, and in the Lord
यज्ज्ञात्वा न पुनर्मोहमेवं यास्यसि पाण्डव ।
येन भूतान्यशेषेण द्रक्ष्यस्यात्मन्यथो मयि ॥ ४.३५ ॥
yaj jñātvā na punar moham evaṁ yāsyasi pāṇḍava |
yena bhūtāny aśeṣeṇa drakṣyasy ātmany atho mayi ||
"Knowing which, you will not again fall into delusion; by which you will see all beings, without exception, in yourself, and then in Me."
Bhāṣya: The fruit of this knowledge: permanent end of delusion, and the vision of all beings within the Self, and the Self within the Lord — the realization of non-dual identity (samyag-darśana).
BG 4.36The raft of knowledge crosses all sin
अपि चेदसि पापेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यः पापकृत्तमः ।
सर्वं ज्ञानप्लवेनैव वृजिनं सन्तरिष्यसि ॥ ४.३६ ॥
api ced asi pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ pāpa-kṛt-tamaḥ |
sarvaṁ jñāna-plavenaiva vṛjinaṁ santariṣyasi ||
"Even if you were the most sinful of all sinners, you would cross over all wickedness by the raft of knowledge alone."
Bhāṣya: The unmatched power of knowledge: even the worst sinner can cross the entire ocean of saṁsāra by the "raft of knowledge," which destroys the very root (ignorance) from which all sins spring.
BG 4.37⭐ The fire of knowledge burns all karma to ashes
यथैधांसि समिद्धोऽग्निर्भस्मसात्कुरुतेऽर्जुन ।
ज्ञानाग्निः सर्वकर्माणि भस्मसात्कुरुते तथा ॥ ४.३७ ॥
yathaidhāṁsi samiddho 'gnir bhasmasāt kurute 'rjuna |
jñānāgniḥ sarva-karmāṇi bhasmasāt kurute tathā ||
"As a blazing fire reduces firewood to ashes, so does the fire of knowledge reduce all actions to ashes."
Bhāṣya: The famous metaphor: as fire burns fuel to ash, the fire of knowledge burns up all accumulated karma (except the prārabdha already begun) — reducing its binding power to nothing. This is why knowledge, not action, liberates.
BG 4.38Nothing purifies like knowledge — found within
न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते ।
तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति ॥ ४.३८ ॥
na hi jñānena sadṛśaṁ pavitram iha vidyate |
tat svayaṁ yoga-saṁsiddhaḥ kālenātmani vindati ||
"There is nothing here as purifying as knowledge. One perfected in yoga finds it in himself, in time, of his own accord."
Bhāṣya: Nothing purifies like knowledge — the supreme pavitra. And it is not external: one perfected by karma-yoga (purified in mind) discovers this knowledge within his own Self in due course. Karma-yoga ripens; knowledge dawns from within.
BG 4.39Faith → knowledge → supreme peace
श्रद्धावाँल्लभते ज्ञानं तत्परः संयतेन्द्रियः ।
ज्ञानं लब्ध्वा परां शान्तिमचिरेणाधिगच्छति ॥ ४.३९ ॥
śraddhāvāl labhate jñānaṁ tat-paraḥ saṁyatendriyaḥ |
jñānaṁ labdhvā parāṁ śāntim acireṇādhigacchati ||
"The man of faith, devoted to it, with senses restrained, gains knowledge; having gained knowledge, he swiftly attains supreme peace."
Bhāṣya: The qualifications for knowledge: śraddhā (faith), tat-para (single-minded devotion), saṁyatendriya (sense-control). Such a one gains knowledge and quickly reaches supreme peace (parā śānti). Faith is the first requisite.
BG 4.40The doubter is ruined
अज्ञश्चाश्रद्दधानश्च संशयात्मा विनश्यति ।
नायं लोकोऽस्ति न परो न सुखं संशयात्मनः ॥ ४.४० ॥
ajñaś cāśraddadhānaś ca saṁśayātmā vinaśyati |
nāyaṁ loko 'sti na paro na sukhaṁ saṁśayātmanaḥ ||
"The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting one perishes. For the doubting soul there is neither this world, nor the next, nor happiness."
Bhāṣya: The opposite of the faithful seeker. Doubt is the worst — the doubting mind (saṁśayātmā) enjoys neither this world, nor the next, nor any happiness. Doubt paralyzes and destroys.
BG 4.41⭐ Renounced action, doubts cut, poised in the Self
योगसंन्यस्तकर्माणं ज्ञानसञ्छिन्नसंशयम् ।
आत्मवन्तं न कर्माणि निबध्नन्ति धनञ्जय ॥ ४.४१ ॥
yoga-saṁnyasta-karmāṇaṁ jñāna-sañchinna-saṁśayam |
ātmavantaṁ na karmāṇi nibadhnanti dhanañjaya ||
"Actions do not bind him who has renounced action through yoga, whose doubts are cut asunder by knowledge, and who is poised in the Self, O Dhanañjaya."
Bhāṣya: The perfected one, by three marks: (1) actions renounced through yoga/knowledge; (2) all doubts severed by knowledge; (3) established in the Self — is not bound by any action. This ties together action-renunciation-through-knowledge — the very title of the chapter.
BG 4.42⭐ Cut doubt with the sword of knowledge — arise!
तस्मादज्ञानसम्भूतं हृत्स्थं ज्ञानासिनात्मनः ।
छित्त्वैनं संशयं योगमातिष्ठोत्तिष्ठ भारत ॥ ४.४२ ॥
tasmād ajñāna-sambhūtaṁ hṛt-sthaṁ jñānāsinātmanaḥ |
chittvainaṁ saṁśayaṁ yogam ātiṣṭhottiṣṭha bhārata ||
"Therefore, with the sword of knowledge cut this doubt of yours, born of ignorance and seated in the heart; take refuge in yoga. Arise, O Bhārata!"
Bhāṣya: The ringing conclusion. With the "sword of knowledge" (jñāna-asi), sever the doubt that ignorance has planted in the heart; then, established in yoga, "Arise!" — get up and fight. Knowledge cuts doubt, and the purified seeker returns to action.
🌟 The arc of Chapter 4 in one breath (Śaṅkara's reading)

This yoga is ancient and divine (4.1–3) → the Lord incarnates through māyā, remaining the actionless non-doer (4.5–15) → the summit: "see inaction in action," and let action be burnt in the fire of knowledge (4.16–24) → all sacrifices are forms of action, but the sacrifice of knowledge is highest, for all action culminates in knowledge (4.25–33) → so seek this knowledge from a guru; it purifies, burns karma, grants peace — cut your doubt and arise (4.34–42).

The renunciation taught here is internal and epistemic: not dropping activity, but dropping the false "I am the doer" by knowing the Self.
→ Continues in Chapter 5 (Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga): Chapter 4 taught renunciation of agency through knowledge. Arjuna, now hearing both "act!" and "renounce through knowledge," will ask directly in 5.1: which is better — renunciation of action, or the yoga of action? Chapter 5 answers and reconciles the two.
