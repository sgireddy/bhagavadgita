ॐ Chapter 2 — Sāṅkhya Yoga ॐ

साङ्ख्ययोगः : సాంఖ్యయోగము
"The Yoga of Analysis / Knowledge"  ·  72 verses  ·  The Gītā in miniature
If you only read one chapter of the Gītā, read this one. Many traditions hold that the entire teaching of the Gītā is contained in Chapter 2 — the rest only expands it. Kṛṣṇa begins by answering Arjuna's despair with the most fundamental truth:

"You are not the body. You are the eternal Self (ātman) — never born, never dying."

From that ground He builds the entire path of Karma-Yoga — selfless action — and ends by painting the timeless portrait of the sthita-prajña, the person of steady wisdom.

⭐ Other essential verses of Chapter 2

BG 2.20 The eternity of the Self — never born, never dying
न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्
नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो
न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ २.२० ॥
న జాయతే మ్రియతే వా కదాచిన్
నాయం భూత్వా భవితా వా న భూయః ।
అజో నిత్యః శాశ్వతోఽయం పురాణో
న హన్యతే హన్యమానే శరీరే ॥ ౨.౨౦ ॥
na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin
nāyaṁ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ |
ajo nityaḥ śāśvato 'yaṁ purāṇo
na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre ||
"It is not born, nor does it ever die.
Having been, it will never cease to be.
Unborn, eternal, everlasting, ancient —
it is not slain when the body is slain."
Echoed in the Kaṭha Upaniṣad. The Self is what the body is not.

BG 2.22 The garment simile — the soul changes bodies as one changes clothes
वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय
नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि ।
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णान्य्
अन्यानि संयाति नवानि देही ॥ २.२२ ॥
వాసాంసి జీర్ణాని యథా విహాయ
నవాని గృహ్ణాతి నరోఽపరాణి ।
తథా శరీరాణి విహాయ జీర్ణాన్య్
అన్యాని సంయాతి నవాని దేహీ ॥ ౨.౨౨ ॥
vāsāṁsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya
navāni gṛhṇāti naro 'parāṇi |
tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇāny
anyāni saṁyāti navāni dehī ||
"Just as a person casts off worn-out clothes
and puts on new ones,
so the embodied Self casts off worn-out bodies
and enters new ones."
One of the most beloved verses in all Indian thought.
BG 2.62–63 The famous chain of downfall — from brooding to ruin
ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते ।
सङ्गात्सञ्जायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥
क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः ।
स्मृतिभ्रंशाद्बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥
"Brooding on objects → attachment → desire → anger → delusion →
loss of memory → loss of intellect → ruin."
One of the most-quoted causal cascades in Indian philosophy. Full word-by-word in sthita_prajna.html.
🌟 Essential take-away of Chapter 2

You are the eternal Self.   Act without attachment to results.   Be even-minded in success and failure.

If a person could remember just three Sanskrit phrases from the entire Gītā, these three from Chapter 2 would carry the whole teaching:

1. na jāyate mriyate vā kadācit (2.20) — The Self is never born and never dies.
2. karmaṇy evādhikāras te (2.47) — Your right is to action alone.
3. samatvaṁ yoga ucyate (2.48) — Equanimity itself is yoga.
