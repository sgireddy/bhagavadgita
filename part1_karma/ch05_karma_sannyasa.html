ॐ Chapter 5 — Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga ॐ

कर्मसंन्यासयोगः : కర్మసన్యాసయోగము
"The Yoga of Renunciation of Action"  ·  29 verses  ·  Renounce in action, not from it
Arjuna asks again: "Which is better — to renounce action or to perform it?"

Kṛṣṇa's answer in Ch 5 is decisive: both paths reach the same goal, but karma-yoga (action without ego) is easier than karma-sannyāsa (giving up action altogether). The wise one is the one who acts without claiming the action as his own — who sees the same Self in all beings, and who lives free of attraction and aversion. This chapter introduces the brahma-bhūtaḥ sage — one who has become Brahman.

⭐ Essential verses of Chapter 5

BG 5.18 Equal vision — the wise see the same Self in all
विद्याविनयसम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि ।
शुनि चैव श्वपाके च पण्डिताः समदर्शिनः ॥ ५.१८ ॥
విద్యావినయసంపన్నే బ్రాహ్మణే గవి హస్తిని ।
శుని చైవ శ్వపాకే చ పండితాః సమదర్శినః ॥ ౫.౧౮ ॥
vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini |
śuni caiva śva-pāke ca paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ ||
"In a learned and humble brāhmaṇa, in a cow, in an elephant,
in a dog, and in a dog-eater — the wise see the same."
samadarśinaḥ — equal vision: the deliberate refusal to classify beings as higher and lower.

BG 5.22 Sensory pleasures are wombs of misery — the wise don't rest in them
ये हि संस्पर्शजा भोगा दुःखयोनय एव ते ।
आद्यन्तवन्तः कौन्तेय न तेषु रमते बुधः ॥ ५.२२ ॥
యే హి సంస్పర్శజా భోగా దుఃఖయోనయ ఏవ తే ।
ఆద్యంతవంతః కౌంతేయ న తేషు రమతే బుధః ॥ ౫.౨౨ ॥
ye hi saṁsparśa-jā bhogā duḥkha-yonaya eva te |
ādy-antavantaḥ kaunteya na teṣu ramate budhaḥ ||
"The pleasures born of sense-contact are wombs of suffering —
they have a beginning and an end, O Kaunteya.
The wise do not delight in them."
BG 5.29 The recipe for peace — closing verse of the chapter
भोक्तारं यज्ञतपसां सर्वलोकमहेश्वरम् ।
सुहृदं सर्वभूतानां ज्ञात्वा मां शान्तिमृच्छति ॥ ५.२९ ॥
భోక్తారం యజ్ఞతపసాం సర్వలోకమహేశ్వరమ్ ।
సుహృదం సర్వభూతానాం జ్ఞాత్వా మాం శాంతిమృచ్ఛతి ॥ ౫.౨౯ ॥
bhoktāraṁ yajña-tapasāṁ sarva-loka-maheśvaram |
suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānāṁ jñātvā māṁ śāntim ṛcchati ||
"Knowing Me as the enjoyer of all sacrifices and austerities,
the great Lord of all the worlds,
the friend of all beings — one attains peace."
suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānām — "the friend of all beings" — one of the most tender phrases in the Gītā.
🌟 Essential take-away of Chapter 5

Renounce inside, not outside.
True renunciation is not abandoning the work; it is abandoning the ego-claim on the work. The sage acts but says, "I am not doing — the senses move among sense-objects."

Three marks of the inner sage:
1. Equal vision (5.18) — sees the same Self in everyone, from priest to outcaste.
2. Inner freedom (5.22) — doesn't seek lasting joy in fleeting pleasures.
3. Knowing the Lord as friend (5.29) — sees the Divine as suhṛd, friend of all.
