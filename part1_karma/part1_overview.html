Action & the Self — Chapters 1 to 6 of the Bhagavad Gītā
The first six chapters of the Gītā answer one essential question: "Who am I, and what should I do?"
They form the karma-kāṇḍa — the section on action and the self. Across these six "yogas," Arjuna is shown that he is not the body but the eternal Self (ātman), and that the way to live in this world is through selfless, disciplined action (karma-yoga) performed with equanimity (samatva), not attachment to results. The journey culminates in meditation (dhyāna), where the mind becomes "like a lamp in a windless place" — steady, undisturbed.

📜 The six yogas of Part I — at a glance

Ch Name (Yoga) 🌟 Essential take-away
1 Arjuna-Viṣāda Yoga
अर्जुनविषादयोगः
అర్జునవిషాదయోగము
"Arjuna's Despondency"		 The spiritual journey often begins with a crisis — paralysis, doubt, grief. That collapse is not failure; it is the door.
Arjuna's collapse on the battlefield mirrors every honest seeker's first moment of "I cannot do this anymore."
2 Sāṅkhya Yoga
साङ्ख्ययोगः
సాంఖ్యయోగము
"Yoga of Analysis"		 The Self (ātman) is eternal and indestructible; only the body dies. Act from your duty (svadharma) without attachment to fruits. "samatvaṁ yoga ucyate" — equanimity itself is yoga.
(The Gītā in miniature — many traditions say if you only read one chapter, read this one.)
3 Karma Yoga
कर्मयोगः
కర్మయోగము
"Yoga of Action"		 You cannot escape action — even breathing is action. The solution isn't renunciation of work but renunciation of selfish motive. Act as yajña (sacrifice / offering), for the welfare of all (loka-saṅgraha).
4 Jñāna-Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga
ज्ञानकर्मसंन्यासयोगः
జ్ఞానకర్మసన్యాసయోగము
"Knowledge & Renunciation of Action"		 When dharma declines, the Lord takes birth, age after age (yadā yadā hi dharmasya…). Action illumined by knowledge — seeing inaction in action — does not bind.
5 Karma-Sannyāsa Yoga
कर्मसंन्यासयोगः
కర్మసన్యాసయోగము
"Renunciation of Action"		 Both paths lead to the same goal, but action without ego is easier and equally liberating as renunciation. The wise one acts but doesn't claim doership.
6 Dhyāna Yoga
ध्यानयोगः
ధ్యానయోగము
"Yoga of Meditation"		 Be your own friend, not your own enemy. Through steady meditation and moderation (food, sleep, work) the restless mind becomes still — "like a lamp in a windless place" (6.19).
🎯 The arc of Part I  ·  Crisis (Ch 1) → Self-knowledge (Ch 2) → Selfless action (Ch 3–5) → Meditative stillness (Ch 6).

By the end of Chapter 6, Arjuna has been given a complete contemplative path: he knows what he truly is, how to act in the world, and how to still his mind. He is now ready to be shown the Divine itself — which is the work of Part II (Ch 7–12).

⭐ The essential verses of Part I (one per chapter)

BG 1.1 Ch 1 · The opening verse of the entire Gītā — Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks Sañjaya what happened on the battlefield
धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः ।
मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय ॥ १.१ ॥
ధర్మక్షేత్రే కురుక్షేత్రే సమవేతా యుయుత్సవః ।
మామకాః పాండవాశ్చైవ కిమకుర్వత సంజయ ॥ ౧.౧ ॥
dharma-kṣetre kuru-kṣetre samavetā yuyutsavaḥ |
māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāś caiva kim akurvata sañjaya ||
"On the field of dharma, at Kurukṣetra — gathered, eager to fight —
what did my sons and the Pāṇḍavas do, O Sañjaya?"
The Gītā opens with the word dharma. The entire epic of 700 verses is framed as dharma-kṣetra — "the field of right action." Every human life is a Kurukṣetra.
BG 2.47 Ch 2 · The single most-quoted verse of the entire Gītā — the foundation of Karma-Yoga
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ २.४७ ॥
కర్మణ్యేవాధికారస్తే మా ఫలేషు కదాచన ।
మా కర్మఫలహేతుర్భూర్మా తే సంగోఽస్త్వకర్మణి ॥ ౨.౪౭ ॥
karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana |
mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi ||
"You have the right to action alone, never to its fruits.
Be not the cause of the fruit of action; nor let your attachment be to inaction."

BG 3.35 Ch 3 · The doctrine of svadharma — your own duty, however imperfect
श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात् ।
स्वधर्मे निधनं श्रेयः परधर्मो भयावहः ॥ ३.३५ ॥
శ్రేయాన్స్వధర్మో విగుణః పరధర్మాత్స్వనుష్ఠితాత్ ।
స్వధర్మే నిధనం శ్రేయః పరధర్మో భయావహః ॥ ౩.౩౫ ॥
śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt |
sva-dharme nidhanaṁ śreyaḥ para-dharmo bhayāvahaḥ ||
"Better is one's own duty, though imperfectly performed,
than another's duty well performed.
Better is death in one's own duty — another's duty is fraught with fear."

BG 4.7 Ch 4 · The principle of avatāra — divine descent
यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत ।
अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥ ४.७ ॥
యదా యదా హి ధర్మస్య గ్లానిర్భవతి భారత ।
అభ్యుత్థానమధర్మస్య తదాత్మానం సృజామ్యహమ్ ॥ ౪.౭ ॥
yadā yadā hi dharmasya glānir bhavati bhārata |
abhyutthānam adharmasya tadātmānaṁ sṛjāmy aham ||
"Whenever there is decline of righteousness, O Bhārata,
and rise of unrighteousness — then I manifest Myself."

BG 4.38 Ch 4 · Nothing in this world purifies like knowledge
न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते ।
तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति ॥ ४.३८ ॥
న హి జ్ఞానేన సదృశం పవిత్రమిహ విద్యతే ।
తత్స్వయం యోగసంసిద్ధః కాలేనాత్మని విందతి ॥ ౪.౩౮ ॥
na hi jñānena sadṛśaṁ pavitram iha vidyate |
tat svayaṁ yoga-saṁsiddhaḥ kālenātmani vindati ||
"In this world, there is nothing as purifying as knowledge.
The one perfected in yoga finds it of himself, in himself, in time."
BG 5.29 Ch 5 · The recipe for peace — know the Lord as the friend of all beings
भोक्तारं यज्ञतपसां सर्वलोकमहेश्वरम् ।
सुहृदं सर्वभूतानां ज्ञात्वा मां शान्तिमृच्छति ॥ ५.२९ ॥
భోక్తారం యజ్ఞతపసాం సర్వలోకమహేశ్వరమ్ ।
సుహృదం సర్వభూతానాం జ్ఞాత్వా మాం శాంతిమృచ్ఛతి ॥ ౫.౨౯ ॥
bhoktāraṁ yajña-tapasāṁ sarva-loka-maheśvaram |
suhṛdaṁ sarva-bhūtānāṁ jñātvā māṁ śāntim ṛcchati ||
"Knowing Me as the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities,
the great Lord of all the worlds, the friend of all beings —
one attains peace."
BG 6.19 Ch 6 · The lamp-in-a-windless-place simile — the steady meditative mind
यथा दीपो निवातस्थो नेङ्गते सोपमा स्मृता ।
योगिनो यतचित्तस्य युञ्जतो योगमात्मनः ॥ ६.१९ ॥
యథా దీపో నివాతస్థో నేంగతే సోపమా స్మృతా ।
యోగినో యతచిత్తస్య యుంజతో యోగమాత్మనః ॥ ౬.౧౯ ॥
yathā dīpo nivāta-stho neṅgate sopamā smṛtā |
yogino yata-cittasya yuñjato yogam ātmanaḥ ||
"As a lamp in a windless place does not flicker —
so is the simile used for the disciplined mind of the yogi
who practices the yoga of the Self."
