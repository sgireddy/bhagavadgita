ॐ Chapter 1 — Arjuna-Viṣāda Yoga ॐ

अर्जुनविषादयोगः : అర్జునవిషాదయోగము
"The Yoga of Arjuna's Despondency"  ·  47 verses  ·  The opening of the Bhagavad Gītā
The Gītā does not begin with a teaching — it begins with a breakdown.
Standing between the two armies at Kurukṣetra, Arjuna — the greatest warrior of his age — looks across at his teachers, cousins, uncles, and friends, and his hands drop. His bow slips. He cannot fight. He sits down on the chariot, overwhelmed.

This collapse is not weakness. It is the honest moment that every seeker must reach before real wisdom can be received. Chapter 1 sets up the entire Gītā as Kṛṣṇa's response to Arjuna's despair — and by extension, the response to every human being who has stood at a moral crossroads and asked, "What should I do?"

⭐ Essential verses of Chapter 1

BG 1.1 The very first verse — Dhṛtarāṣṭra asks Sañjaya what is happening on the battlefield
धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः ।
मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय ॥ १.१ ॥
ధర్మక్షేత్రే కురుక్షేత్రే సమవేతా యుయుత్సవః ।
మామకాః పాండవాశ్చైవ కిమకుర్వత సంజయ ॥ ౧.౧ ॥
dharma-kṣetre kuru-kṣetre samavetā yuyutsavaḥ |
māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāś caiva kim akurvata sañjaya ||
"On the field of dharma, at Kurukṣetra — gathered, eager to fight —
what did my sons and the Pāṇḍavas do, O Sañjaya?"
The Gītā opens with the word dharma. The battle isn't just military — it's moral. Every human life is a dharma-kṣetra.

BG 1.30 Arjuna's body and mind begin to fail him
न च शक्नोम्यवस्थातुं भ्रमतीव च मे मनः ।
निमित्तानि च पश्यामि विपरीतानि केशव ॥ १.३० ॥
న చ శక్నోమ్యవస్థాతుం భ్రమతీవ చ మే మనః ।
నిమిత్తాని చ పశ్యామి విపరీతాని కేశవ ॥ ౧.౩౦ ॥
na ca śaknomy avasthātuṁ bhramatīva ca me manaḥ |
nimittāni ca paśyāmi viparītāni keśava ||
"I am not able to stand. My mind is reeling.
I see only evil omens, O Keśava."
The body fails before the soul speaks. Real questions come when our usual capacity collapses.
BG 1.47 The chapter's final image — Arjuna sets down his bow
सञ्जय उवाच ।
एवमुक्त्वार्जुनः सङ्ख्ये रथोपस्थ उपाविशत् ।
विसृज्य सशरं चापं शोकसंविग्नमानसः ॥ १.४७ ॥
సంజయ ఉవాచ ।
ఏవముక్త్వార్జునః సంఖ్యే రథోపస్థ ఉపావిశత్ ।
విసృజ్య సశరం చాపం శోకసంవిగ్నమానసః ॥ ౧.౪౭ ॥
sañjaya uvāca |
evam uktvārjunaḥ saṅkhye rathopastha upāviśat |
visṛjya sa-śaraṁ cāpaṁ śoka-saṁvigna-mānasaḥ ||
"Sañjaya said: Having spoken thus on the battlefield,
Arjuna sat down on the seat of the chariot,
casting aside his bow and arrows, his mind overwhelmed with grief."
This is where the Gītā truly begins. The warrior cannot fight. The Lord must now speak.
🌟 Essential take-away of Chapter 1

The spiritual journey begins not with answers, but with an honest collapse.
Arjuna's despondency (viṣāda) is paradoxically called a yoga — because his honest "I cannot do this" is itself the first sacred act. Without it, there would be no Gītā.

"When you reach the place where your own strength runs out — that is where God begins to speak."

Three lessons:
1. Every life is a dharma-kṣetra — a field of moral choice.
2. Doubt and grief, honestly faced, become the door to wisdom.
3. The student must sit down and surrender before the teacher can stand and speak.
