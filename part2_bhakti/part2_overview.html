The Divine & Devotion — Chapters 7 to 12 of the Bhagavad Gītā
Chapters 7–12 answer the second great question: "Who is God, and how do I relate to Him?"
Where Part I taught the seeker who he is, Part II turns the gaze outward and upward — to the Divine itself. Kṛṣṇa is no longer only the friend on the chariot; He gradually unveils Himself as the source of all that exists, the cosmic-form devourer of worlds, and finally — the intimately personal beloved who can be reached by simple, sincere love.

The arc moves from cosmic wonder to tender devotion (bhakti). It is the section that gives the Gītā its heart.

📜 The six yogas of Part II — at a glance

Ch Name (Yoga) 🌟 Essential take-away
7 Jñāna-Vijñāna Yoga
ज्ञानविज्ञानयोगः
జ్ఞానవిజ్ఞానయోగము
"Knowledge & Realization"		 The Lord is both the manifest (lower nature) and the unmanifest (higher nature) reality — "the string on which all beads are strung" (7.7). Four kinds of people approach Him: the distressed, the seeker, the wealth-seeker, and the wise.
8 Akṣara-Brahma Yoga
अक्षरब्रह्मयोगः
అక్షరబ్రహ్మయోగము
"The Imperishable Brahman"		 Whatever you remember at the moment of death — that you become (8.6). Therefore: remember the Divine always (8.7). Death isn't terrifying when consciousness is already trained.
9 Rāja-Vidyā Rāja-Guhya Yoga
राजविद्याराजगुह्ययोगः
రాజవిద్యారాజగుహ్యయోగము
"The Royal Knowledge / Royal Secret"		 Pure, simple devotion (bhakti) is the "king of secrets". "Even a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water — if offered with love, I accept" (9.26). God doesn't care about your sophistication — He cares about your heart.
10 Vibhūti Yoga
विभूतियोगः
విభూతియోగము
"Divine Glories"		 Wherever there is beauty, power, brilliance, or excellence — there I AM. ("Of mountains I am Meru, of rivers the Gaṅgā, of letters I am A…") The world is shot through with divinity; learn to see it.
11 Viśva-Rūpa-Darśana Yoga
विश्वरूपदर्शनयोगः
విశ్వరూపదర్శనయోగము
"Vision of the Cosmic Form"		 Kṛṣṇa reveals His terrifying, all-encompassing cosmic form — galaxies, gods, demons, time devouring all worlds (kālo'smi, 11.32). Arjuna is overwhelmed and asks for the gentle form back. Lesson: God is unimaginably vast — and also intimately personal.
12 Bhakti Yoga
भक्तियोगः
భక్తియోగము
"The Yoga of Devotion"		 The path of loving the personal form is easier for embodied beings; both reach the same goal. The Lord describes His ideal devotee: friendly to all, free from "I" and "mine", even-minded in joy and sorrow — and says "such a devotee is dear to Me".
🎯 The arc of Part II  ·  Knowing the Divine (Ch 7–8) → Simple devotion as supreme path (Ch 9) → Seeing the Divine in all (Ch 10) → Cosmic terror & awe (Ch 11) → Intimate love (Ch 12).

⭐ The essential verses of Part II

BG 7.7 Ch 7 · The string on which all beads are strung
मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय ।
मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव ॥ ७.७ ॥
మత్తః పరతరం నాన్యత్కించిదస్తి ధనంజయ ।
మయి సర్వమిదం ప్రోతం సూత్రే మణిగణా ఇవ ॥ ౭.౭ ॥
mattaḥ parataraṁ nānyat kiñcid asti dhanañjaya |
mayi sarvam idaṁ protaṁ sūtre maṇi-gaṇā iva ||
"There is nothing higher than Me, O Dhanañjaya.
All this is strung on Me — like beads on a thread."
BG 8.7 Ch 8 · Therefore remember Me always — and fight
तस्मात्सर्वेषु कालेषु मामनुस्मर युध्य च ।
मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्मामेवैष्यस्यसंशयम् ॥ ८.७ ॥
తస్మాత్సర్వేషు కాలేషు మామనుస్మర యుధ్య చ ।
మయ్యర్పితమనోబుద్ధిర్మామేవైష్యస్యసంశయమ్ ॥ ౮.౭ ॥
tasmāt sarveṣu kāleṣu mām anusmara yudhya ca |
mayy arpita-mano-buddhir mām evaiṣyasy asaṁśayam ||
"Therefore at all times remember Me — and fight.
With mind and intellect dedicated to Me, you shall come to Me alone — without doubt."
BG 9.26 Ch 9 · The most beloved verse in the entire Bhakti tradition
पत्रं पुष्पं फलं तोयं यो मे भक्त्या प्रयच्छति ।
तदहं भक्त्युपहृतमश्नामि प्रयतात्मनः ॥ ९.२६ ॥
పత్రం పుష్పం ఫలం తోయం యో మే భక్త్యా ప్రయచ్ఛతి ।
తదహం భక్త్యుపహృతమశ్నామి ప్రయతాత్మనః ॥ ౯.౨౬ ॥
patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati |
tad ahaṁ bhakty-upahṛtam aśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ ||
"A leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water —
whoever offers it to Me with love
— that I accept, offered with devotion by the pure-hearted."
BG 11.32 Ch 11 · The most quoted line of the Gītā in the modern world (Oppenheimer)
कालोऽस्मि लोकक्षयकृत्प्रवृद्धो
लोकान्समाहर्तुमिह प्रवृत्तः ॥ (११.३२)
కాలోఽస్మి లోకక్షయకృత్ప్రవృద్ధో
లోకాన్సమాహర్తుమిహ ప్రవృత్తః ॥ (౧౧.౩౨)
kālo 'smi loka-kṣaya-kṛt pravṛddho
lokān samāhartum iha pravṛttaḥ ||
"I am Time, the mighty destroyer of worlds —
here come forth to annihilate the worlds."
Quoted by J. Robert Oppenheimer on witnessing the first atomic bomb test (Trinity, 1945).
BG 12.13–14 Ch 12 · The portrait of the devotee dear to the Lord
अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च ।
निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी ॥
सन्तुष्टः सततं योगी यतात्मा दृढनिश्चयः ।
मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः ॥
"Free from hatred toward any being, friendly and compassionate,
without 'mine'-ness or ego, equal in pain and pleasure, forgiving,
ever content, disciplined, of firm resolve,
with mind and intellect surrendered to Me — such a devotee is dear to Me."
💎 If you remember just three lines from all of Part II:

1. sūtre maṇi-gaṇā iva (7.7) — all things are strung on the Divine like beads on a thread.
2. patraṁ puṣpaṁ phalaṁ toyaṁ (9.26) — a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water — offered with love, He accepts.
3. advēṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṁ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca (12.13) — the devotee dear to God is friendly to all.
