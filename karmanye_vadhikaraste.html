Bhagavad Gītā · Chapter 2 · Verse 47 — Lord Kṛṣṇa to Arjuna · The essence of Karma-Yoga
Verse 2.47 — The most famous śloka of the Gītā
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥
కర్మణ్యేవాధికారస్తే మా ఫలేషు కదాచన ।
మా కర్మఫలహేతుర్భూర్మా తే సంగోऽస్త్వకర్మణి ॥
karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana
mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo’stv akarmaṇi
“You have the right to perform your duty alone — but never to its fruits. Let not the fruits of action be your motive, nor let your attachment be to inaction.”

1️⃣ Right to ActionYou are entitled to do your duty.
2️⃣ Not to FruitsNever claim ownership of results.
3️⃣ No Motive of RewardDon’t act because of the fruit.
4️⃣ Nor InactionAnd don’t cling to doing nothing.
📖 Combined meaning: “Your authority is over action alone — never over its fruits. Do not let the result be your motive, yet do not become attached to inaction either.”  This is the foundational verse of Niṣkāma Karma-Yoga — the path of selfless, dedicated action. Kṛṣṇa teaches Arjuna that freedom lies not in renouncing work, but in renouncing the anxiety of its outcome. Act fully, surrender the fruit.
