ॐ Chapter 18 — Mokṣa-Sannyāsa Yoga ॐ

मोक्षसंन्यासयोगः : మోక్షసన్యాసయోగము
"The Yoga of Liberation through Renunciation"  ·  78 verses  ·  The longest chapter — the grand finale
Chapter 18 is the grand synthesis — the longest chapter of the Gītā (78 verses) — and Kṛṣṇa weaves together every thread He has been teaching across all 17 preceding chapters. He defines true renunciation, analyzes action under the three guṇas, describes the four divisions of human nature (varṇa) by aptitude, and reaffirms the supremacy of svadharma. Then He builds — verse by verse — toward what many traditions hold to be the single most important verse in the entire Gītā: 18.66, the charama-śloka (the final / supreme verse) — the call to total surrender.

The Gītā closes with Sañjaya's promise that wherever Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna are together — there will be victory, prosperity, and lasting righteousness.

⭐ Essential verses of Chapter 18

BG 18.46 Worship through one's own work — work as offering
यतः प्रवृत्तिर्भूतानां येन सर्वमिदं ततम् ।
स्वकर्मणा तमभ्यर्च्य सिद्धिं विन्दति मानवः ॥ १८.४६ ॥
యతః ప్రవృత్తిర్భూతానాం యేన సర్వమిదం తతమ్ ।
స్వకర్మణా తమభ్యర్చ్య సిద్ధిం విందతి మానవః ॥ ౧౮.౪౬ ॥
yataḥ pravṛttir bhūtānāṁ yena sarvam idaṁ tatam |
sva-karmaṇā tam abhyarcya siddhiṁ vindati mānavaḥ ||
"By worshipping Him — from whom all beings arise,
by whom all this is pervaded —
through one's own work,
a person attains perfection."
The principle that any honest work, offered with the right attitude, becomes a path to God.
BG 18.47 Echo of 3.35 — better one's own duty imperfectly than another's well
श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात् ।
स्वभावनियतं कर्म कुर्वन्नाप्नोति किल्बिषम् ॥ १८.४७ ॥
śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt |
svabhāva-niyataṁ karma kurvan nāpnoti kilbiṣam ||
"Better is one's own duty, though imperfect,
than another's duty well performed.
By doing the work prescribed by one's own nature,
one incurs no sin."
BG 18.61 The Lord dwells in the heart, spinning all beings — like figures on a machine
ईश्वरः सर्वभूतानां हृद्देशेऽर्जुन तिष्ठति ।
भ्रामयन्सर्वभूतानि यन्त्रारूढानि मायया ॥ १८.६१ ॥
ఈశ్వరః సర్వభూతానాం హృద్దేశేఽర్జున తిష్ఠతి ।
భ్రామయన్సర్వభూతాని యంత్రారూఢాని మాయయా ॥ ౧౮.౬౧ ॥
īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānāṁ hṛd-deśe 'rjuna tiṣṭhati |
bhrāmayan sarva-bhūtāni yantrārūḍhāni māyayā ||
"The Lord, O Arjuna, dwells in the hearts of all beings —
spinning them all by His māyā,
as if mounted on a wheel of machinery."

✨ The closing verses — and the supreme verse of the Gītā

BG 18.65 Just before the charama-śloka — "I promise you, you are dear to Me"
मन्मना भव मद्भक्तो मद्याजी मां नमस्कुरु ।
मामेवैष्यसि सत्यं ते प्रतिजाने प्रियोऽसि मे ॥ १८.६५ ॥
మన్మనా భవ మద్భక్తో మద్యాజీ మాం నమస్కురు ।
మామేవైష్యసి సత్యం తే ప్రతిజానే ప్రియోఽసి మే ॥ ౧౮.౬౫ ॥
man-manā bhava mad-bhakto mad-yājī māṁ namaskuru |
mām evaiṣyasi satyaṁ te pratijāne priyo 'si me ||
"Fix your mind on Me; be My devotee; sacrifice to Me; bow to Me.
You shall come to Me — I promise you truly — for you are dear to Me."
A rare verse where the Lord swears, by His own word, of His love for the devotee.
★ BG 18.66 — Charama-Śloka ⭐ The supreme, final, and most-quoted verse of the entire Bhagavad Gītā
सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज ।
अहं त्वा सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥ १८.६६ ॥
సర్వధర్మాన్పరిత్యజ్య మామేకం శరణం వ్రజ ।
అహం త్వా సర్వపాపేభ్యో మోక్షయిష్యామి మా శుచః ॥ ౧౮.౬౬ ॥
sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śaraṇaṁ vraja |
ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ ||
"Abandoning all dharmas,
take refuge in Me alone.
I shall liberate you from all sins.
Do not grieve."

Mahatma Gandhi called this "the supreme verse of the Gītā". For Vaiṣṇava traditions (Rāmānuja, Madhva), this is the entire teaching of the Gītā in a single verse.

BG 18.73 Arjuna's answer — the journey of the seeker, complete
अर्जुन उवाच ।
नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत ।
स्थितोऽस्मि गतसन्देहः करिष्ये वचनं तव ॥ १८.७३ ॥
arjuna uvāca |
naṣṭo mohaḥ smṛtir labdhā tvat-prasādān mayācyuta |
sthito 'smi gata-sandehaḥ kariṣye vacanaṁ tava ||
"Arjuna said: My delusion is destroyed. I have regained my memory by Your grace, O Acyuta.
I stand firm, with my doubts gone. I shall do as You say."
This is the moment of transformation — the entire purpose of the Gītā fulfilled.
BG 18.78 ⭐ The very last verse of the Gītā — Sañjaya's conviction
यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णो यत्र पार्थो धनुर्धरः ।
तत्र श्रीर्विजयो भूतिर्ध्रुवा नीतिर्मतिर्मम ॥ १८.७८ ॥
యత్ర యోగేశ్వరః కృష్ణో యత్ర పార్థో ధనుర్ధరః ।
తత్ర శ్రీర్విజయో భూతిర్ధ్రువా నీతిర్మతిర్మమ ॥ ౧౮.౭౮ ॥
yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇo yatra pārtho dhanur-dharaḥ |
tatra śrīr vijayo bhūtir dhruvā nītir matir mama ||
"Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of Yoga,
and wherever there is Pārtha, the wielder of the bow —
there will be prosperity, victory, well-being, and firm righteousness.
This is my conviction."
So ends the Bhagavad Gītā.
🌟 Essential take-away of Chapter 18 — and of the entire Gītā

"Abandon all dharmas. Take refuge in Me alone. I shall liberate you. Do not grieve."

After 700 verses of analysis, ethics, psychology, cosmology, and devotion, the Gītā's final word is the simplest of all: surrender. Not the surrender of resignation — but the surrender of trust. Lay down all your scattered duties, your endless self-correction, your anxious bargaining with fate. Place yourself in the hands of the Divine. "I will take care of the rest."

And the Gītā does not end there. It ends with Arjuna's response:
naṣṭo mohaḥ — "my delusion is gone"  and  kariṣye vacanaṁ tava — "I will do as You say."

That is the journey the Gītā offers every reader.
